Section 1.1: The Real Data Requirement
Job Description:
As a Head, Alternative Crude Oil Evacuation, I track daily production across multiple zones and evacuation points. Understanding production variability (EDA), comparing performance across zones (hypothesis testing), and forecasting bottlenecks (regression) are essential to optimizing our evacuation logistics and meeting Nigeria’s production targets – OPEC’s allocation of 1.5 million barrels per day (bpd), Nigeria’s 2026 national budget benchmark of 1.84 million bpd and long-term goals of 3 million bpd by 2030. I have a 2026 annual target of evacuation 18 million barrels of crude oil based on the budgeted production volumes. This is achievable through periodic fleet expansion, monitoring fleet costs and ensuring capacity is maintained at minimum 150% production capacity.
Why these five techniques matter to your day-to-day work:
EDA: Distribution of production/evacuation by zone, missing values, outlier detection
Visualisation: Heatmaps of zone performance, time series plots, variance analysis charts
Hypothesis Testing: T-tests comparing zone performance; ANOVA across channels; Chi-squared on capacity constraint violations
Correlation: Relationship between budget and actual, production vs evacuation capacity
Regression: Predict daily actual production from budget, zone, evacuation capacity
Data Source
This dataset comprises daily production and evacuation records extracted from the production tracking system for the period January 1 – May 5, 2026.  
Collection Method: Data were extracted from the production database and consolidated into a single Excel workbook. The export includes:
Daily budget production figure by flow station/zone 
Daily actual production recorded 
Variance (Actual minus Budget)
Evacuation capacity 
Evacuation utilization (monthly evacuation/no of days in month)

Sampling Frame:  The company currently has four (out of seven) functional flow stations. The production and evacuation data were obtained from the four locations, namely 
ALK
CC1
CC2 
CC3. 
These flow stations are grouped into 2 zones, namely 
Zone 1 (CC2 and CC3) 
Zone 2 (ALAK and CC1)
Time Period & Sample Frame:
Time Period: 125 days from January 1 to May 5 2026
Frequency: Daily observations aggregated at end-of-day
Sample Size Justification: I selected 125 daily observations spanning 6 months (Jan 1 – May 5 2026) to capture sufficient variance across seasons and multiple production cycles. This period includes both high-productivity (Jan–March) and constrained (Apr–June) seasons, providing realistic variation for analysis.
125 observations provide sufficient statistical power to:
Detect zone-level differences (ANOVA requires ≥30 obs/group; the data shows ~90/zone)
Test for correlation/regression (need ≥10–15 observations per predictor; this was exceeded)

The dataset consists strictly of authorized internal operational metrics used in compliance with organizational information security policies.
